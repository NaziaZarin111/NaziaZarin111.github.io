Research interests

My work sits at the intersection of bioinformatics and translational medicine: using public omics data and structure-based methods to shorten the path from disease biology to candidate therapy. My master's thesis develops an integrated in silico framework for identifying biomarkers and repurposable drugs in colorectal cancer, supervised by Dr. Shaila Haque.

Drug repurposing & network pharmacology

Mapping approved drugs onto disease modules through drug–gene interaction networks to propose low-cost therapeutic candidates for cancers and infectious disease.

Molecular docking & dynamics

Structure-based validation of candidate compounds — binding-affinity screening followed by molecular dynamics simulation to assess complex stability.

Transcriptomics & biomarker discovery

Differential gene expression analysis of public datasets to nominate diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, from data curation through enrichment and survival context.

Comorbidity mechanisms

Shared molecular signatures between co-occurring diseases — lung cancer & tuberculosis, triple-negative breast cancer & type 2 diabetes, T2D & Parkinson's — as joint therapeutic targets.

The sketchbook

Drawing came before the science. A sketchbook is where I learned to look at structure — and the patience it takes to render a form faithfully by hand turns out to be the same patience a protein model or an interaction network asks for. The two habits still feed each other.

The bookshelf

I read constantly — fiction, science writing, and whatever else follows me home. Choosing which favourites to admit to publicly has proven harder than any enrichment analysis; a properly annotated shelf is forthcoming.
